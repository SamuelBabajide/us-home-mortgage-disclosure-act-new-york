What the data recommends

Three findings a borrower
can act on

1

Lower is not better, and the reason matters

Under 20 percent debt-to-income denies at 24.49 percent, worse than every band from 20 to 49, where the rate is flat near 13. That bottom is not cautious borrowers: it is small second-lien business. The threshold is a line, not a direction. Get under 50. Below 20 buys nothing.

2

Which lender you ask is worth 15 times

Same product in every row, conventional first-lien home purchase, 72 lenders with 2,000 or more such applications. Denial runs 1.14 to 31.57 percent. SEFCU denies 1.14 on more leveraged, lower-income applicants than Bank of America, which denies 17.76. Most borrowers approach one lender.

3

Borrowing against a home you own is a different market

Home improvement denies at 35.13 percent and other purpose at 35.84, against 10.78 for a purchase. With 493 and 479 lenders active rather than 934. Three times as hard, with half the choice.

What this cannot say

The limits, stated
rather than buried

HMDA records what lenders decided and the reason they wrote down. It holds no credit score, no assets and no employment record. So every figure in this carousel is a difference in outcomes, and nothing here isolates the decision that produced it. Three conventions decide most of the numbers.

Purchased loans are excluded everywhere

Action code 6 records a loan a bank bought after another institution made it. The buyer made no decision, so the row belongs in neither the numerator nor the denominator of a rate. 158,743 rows excluded, leaving 1,596,676.

An application ends in one of four ways, and the four sum to 100

Approval, denial, file closed for incompleteness, withdrawal. Reporting all four is what makes several findings visible at all: a lender can reach a low denial rate by deciding well or by losing applications before a decision.

Categories the regulator defines are never dropped

Including the codes for "not available" and "not provided". They are not groups of people, and removing them would change every share in every table without saying so.
